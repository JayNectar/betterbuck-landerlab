I’ve been a homeowner for 4 years. These are the biggest things I wish somebody told me on day one.

By Ben F. | [[month]]/[[day]]/[[year]]

Last updated [[currentdate]]

I thought I had a pretty good idea of what I was getting into when I bought my first home.

Turns out, knowing these things from day one could’ve saved me a lot of money and a few headaches along the way.

1. Please, please look for cheaper home insurance companies once a year. It can save you SO much money

I asked my parents to do this and it literally saved them $1,700/year (and it took around 15 minutes).

Most people have no idea that they're overpaying for home insurance. The avg. household who switches saves $1,034/year.

Stop throwing that money away!

Pull up a site like StopOverpaying.org and it'll do most of the work for you. You enter info about your house and it'll give you some insurance options to look at.

Takes 2-3 minutes, and there are no spam calls, etc.

Try it out.

2. You might also be overpaying for car insurance

I used to just let my car insurance renew every year without thinking much about it. Then I finally checked what else was out there and ended up saving $1,300/year.

Turns out I wasn't the only one overpaying. A study found the average person who switches saves $461/year.²

And you don't have to wait until your policy expires to switch.

I used StopOverpaying.org to check my car insurance options too. It's free, takes about 2 minutes, and there's no obligation to switch.

Worth checking what you could save before your next renewal.

Check your options here.

3. Get yourself a financial advisor

Most people don’t have one, and it’s typically a huge mistake.

Sure, you can manage things on your own if you want to, but most people don’t have the time to actually do things right. There are huge benefits to having somebody pay attention to your money all the time.

People with financial advisors tend to beat the market by ~3%/year (according to a 2019 Vanguard Study). That can make a huge difference over time.

But more important: a good advisor will handle ALL of the annoying retirement stuff & bizarro tax implications you would have never thought of

If you don’t know a financial advisor personally, use a comparison site (like Datalign) and find somebody near you that has good reviews.

Or if you want something easier, here’s a quiz you can fill out that can find an advisor/planner based on your reqs.

4. Your home equity is a gold-mine. If you need funds, stop taking out high-interest loans.

If you own your home but are low on funds, you might want to look into a HELOC (home equity line of credit). Instead of borrowing from your bank, you can essentially borrow from yourself (by tapping into your home's equity).

They may have lower interest rates and more flexible terms that a typical loan would.

Here’s a free page you can use to see how much you could access: link.

5. You don’t have to pay off your debt by yourself

If you’ve got $10k+ in unsecured debt (think credit cards, medical bills, etc), you could use a debt relief program and potentially save up to 45%.

Here’s how to quickly see if you qualify for debt relief:

  • Head to National Debt Relief’s site here
  • See what debt relief options are available
  • Find out if you qualify

Simple as that. You could end up paying less than you owed and be debt free in 24-48 months.

 Here’s a link to National Debt Relief.

6. Stop paying for subscriptions you don't use

We’ve all signed up for free trials and forgotten to cancel them. Stop paying for services you aren’t using!

Take a minute and get yourself a good cancellation app: I like Rocket Money (link here).

It’s an app that will put together a list of your subscriptions so you can pick/choose which ones to cancel.

They also have a premium service that will cancel them for you, if you’d like.

Here’s a link (it’s free).

7. Protect yourself from a huge car repair bill.

If your manufacturer’s warranty has expired, one unexpected repair can get expensive fast.

A vehicle service contract can help with the cost of certain covered repairs, instead of leaving you to deal with the whole bill yourself.

Endurance has different protection plans depending on your vehicle and the level of coverage you’re looking for.

Here’s a page you can use to see what options are available for your car: link

8. Letting IRS debt sit while penalties keep making it more expensive.

Owing the IRS is stressful enough, but penalties and interest can make what you owe even more expensive over time.

Depending on your situation, there may be a few different ways to deal with tax debt.

That could mean setting up a payment plan, seeing if you can get certain penalties reduced, or looking into other ways to deal with what you owe.

If you’re not sure where to start, Five Star Tax Resolution is one company that can help you look at your tax debt and understand what you might be able to do about it.

You’ll share some information about what you owe, and they can help you figure out what to do from there.

Not everyone will qualify for the same kind of help, but it’s worth finding out what you can do rather than letting the debt sit and potentially get more expensive.

See what you might be able to do about your tax debt here

9. Don’t pay for home repairs out of pocket (let a company pay them for you instead).

Picture this: your trusty furnace suddenly throws a tantrum in the dead of winter, leaving you shivering and facing a repair bill that could cost you way more than you anticipated.

If you had a home warranty, you could be covered the next time something breaks down. It’s like having a safety net for your home (think plumbing, electrical, appliances, etc).

If you don’t have one yet, Choice Home Warranty is one of the bigger companies out there.

Bonus: home warranty companies usually have qualified, pre-vetted maintenance and repair workers ready to get the job done (which is one less thing to worry about).

If you’re interested just enter your zip code here to find a home warranty plan. It could save you a bunch of money the next time something breaks down.

10. Sell your house as-is for cash

Listing a home can take time. Repairs, staging, open houses, negotiations - it can drag on for months.

Some homeowners skip that entirely and look for cash buyers instead.

Platforms like Sold.com connect homeowners with investors who may be willing to buy the home as-is. That means no repairs, no showings, and no waiting for bank financing.

In many cases you can see an offer within 1-2 days.
